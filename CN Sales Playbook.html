How we work

Read this once properly. Everything else in the CRM is scaffolding for what's on this page.

What we are

We are a buyer-side advocate. We do not buy, sell, own, lease or resell capacity or hardware — ever. We represent compute buyers, we bring qualified offtakers to operators sitting on uncontracted capacity, and we have a global partner network across power, hardware, networking and financing that helps operators scale. We make money by being useful to both sides, not by taking a position. That is why operators talk to us, and it is the single most important sentence in this document.

The three things a rep does

One

Work your sites

Research, verify, contact and follow up on the sites assigned to you. Nobody else touches them; you own the outcome.

Two

Book the intro call

Your job is a conversation, not a contract. Get the operator on a call. You stay on the deal — the desk joins as support.

Three

Chase the paperwork

NDA, then LOI, then documents signed. Deals die in the gap between interest and signature. Close that gap.

The sequence, every time

1
Qualify before you touch it. Five gates: built and energized, uncontracted MW available now, GPU-ready, a named and verified contact, and a price indication — plus a real source link a human checked. Anything short of that isn't a lead yet.
2
First touch. Open with who we represent. Name their situation. One proof line. One ask: sign the mutual NDA, then buyer requirements follow. Under 150 words. The CRM drafts it; you edit it; you press send.
3
Nudge twice, then stop. Day 3 adds one new angle. Day 8 makes it effortless to close the loop and offers a genuine exit. No guilt, no third chase.
4
NDA back, requirements out. The moment it's signed, send what buyers are actually looking for. Speed here is the whole advantage.
5
Intro call. Log it. Set the next step before you leave the call — always. A deal with no next step is a deal that's already dying.
6
Promote to the Offtake Desk. Mandate → LOI/ICPO → NCNDA → SPA → First Shipment → Recurring. Update the stage the day it moves, not at week's end.

Non-negotiables

✓Say what's true and only what's true. Unknown is a fine answer.
✕Never name a buyer, a fund or a lab. Not even as an example.
✓Set a next step on every deal, every time you touch it.
✕Never imply we own, sell or resell capacity or hardware.
✓Work only your assigned sites. Ask before touching someone else's.
✕No pressure, no false urgency, no chasing a third time.
✓NDA before anything commercially sensitive moves in either direction.
✕No superlatives. If a sentence sounds like marketing, delete it.

What they'll say, and what you say back

Read these out loud once before your first call. They work because they're honest, not because they're clever. When in doubt, be useful and be brief.

"What's your cut? What are you charging me?"

"Nothing changes hands between us to have this conversation. We're engaged on the buyer side — that's who we work for. If a deal comes together we'll be completely transparent about how that works before anything is signed. Right now I'm just trying to find out whether your capacity fits what buyers are actually asking for."

Never dodge this. Answer it in one breath and move to the useful part.
"We already have brokers. We get ten of these a week."

"I believe you, and most of them are shopping your capacity around. We're the other direction — we represent the buyer, so we're not marketing your site to anyone. If what you have doesn't fit, I'll tell you that and stop taking your time."

The differentiator is direction, not effort. Say it plainly and offer the exit.
"Who's the buyer? I'm not signing anything without knowing."

"I can't name them, and honestly you wouldn't want me naming you either — that's exactly what the NDA protects. What I can do straight after it's signed is send you the requirement in detail: capacity, density, term, timing. If that doesn't match your site, we've both saved a week."

Turn the refusal into the reason to sign. Never bend on the name.
"Send me something and I'll take a look."

"Happy to. The NDA's attached — it's two pages and fully mutual. Send it back and I'll have the buyer requirements to you the same day."

"Send me something" is a soft no unless you attach a next step to it.
"Our capacity isn't really available / we're in discussions already."

"Understood — is that all of it, or a portion? Most sites we speak to have a tranche that isn't spoken for. If you're fully contracted, congratulations, and I'll get out of your way. If there's a hall that's still open, that's the conversation."

One question separates a real no from a reflex no.
"Your pricing expectations won't work for us."

"That may be right, and I'd rather find out now. What I'd say is that we're not negotiating against you — we're trying to find the buyer whose requirement matches what you need to earn. Tell me the number that works and I'll be straight with you about whether I can find it."

We advocate hard for our client and stay honest with the operator. Both, always.
"Never heard of you."

"Fair. We're deliberately quiet — most of what we do is under NDA. The fastest way to judge us is the requirement I'll send after the NDA; it'll tell you immediately whether we know this market."

Don't oversell credentials. Offer proof of work instead of a brag.
"Not right now — call me in six months."

"I'll do that. One thing before I go: if a requirement lands that fits your site in the meantime, do you want me to bring it to you, or leave it alone until then?"

Always leave with permission for the next contact, then set the follow-up in the CRM.
If you take one thing

We're not trying to win an argument on the call. We're trying to find out, quickly and honestly, whether we can help. The reps who do best here are the ones who disqualify fast, tell the truth when the answer is no, and are relentlessly useful when the answer is yes.
